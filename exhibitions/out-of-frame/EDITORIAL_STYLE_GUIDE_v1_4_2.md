# Out of Frame — Editorial Voice & Consistency Guide
Version 1.4.2 | 31 October 2026

## Editorial purpose
The camera preserved a selective moment. The artwork broadens the question rather than claiming to restore an unrecorded scene. Visitor copy should invite sustained looking and distinguish the original, related documentation and constructed surrounding image.

## Voice and cadence
- **Observant, precise, quietly questioning.** Begin with a visible detail or a historically documented action. Move from what can be seen to what it reveals or leaves unresolved. Close with a question or considered insight when it is earned.
- **No grandstanding.** Avoid claiming a source image "lied", "erased" someone visibly present or deliberately cropped a recovered wider original. A historical frame can be selective without implying documented intent to exclude any specific person.
- **Short prompts, layered answers.** One interpretive question per chapter; one focused story paragraph; separate tabs for evidence and sources. Maintain complexity where a claim requires it.
- **British English.** Use labour, organised, centre, colour and programme where appropriate. Avoid unverified psychological motives or invented dialogue.
- **Role and person before atmosphere.** Name people when the archive supports an identity, and say when it does not. Never carry names across distinct photographic episodes.
- **Animal life remains central.** Consider the life, death and treatment of the animal alongside human prestige, labour and visual commemoration.

## Shared interface language
- Section: **Research chapters** (nine for Royal Ring, thirteen for Buffalo Corner).
- Reveal: **Reveal nine/thirteen chapters**; hide: **Hide chapter markers**.
- Chapter tabs: **The story** / **Evidence & limits** / **Sources**.
- Visual panel: **Look closer · [ID]**; a short, literal detail heading; **Where this detail sits**; **Find this in the panorama**.
- Global explorer: **Original photograph**, **Begin again**, zoom `− / +`.
- Evidence key: **A** visible in original photograph, **B** documented at event or expedition, **C** contextual evidence, **D** artistic reconstruction. These attach to claims, never the whole scene.

## Distinguish sources
- **Original photograph**: source artefact, intact and separate from the artist's surrounding work.
- **Artwork detail**: crop from the present-day reconstructed panorama; not a historical photograph or a verified historical location.
- **Comparator**: external historical material from another context; not proof of a precise position at the original exposure.
- **Unknown** remains unknown: camera operator, particular workers' names, moments and positions must not be filled in by attractive prose.

## Buffalo Corner's specific limits
The sepia print shows five identified men and three buffalo trophy heads. The surrounding colour scenes combine documented activities, related evidence and artistic reconstruction across different moments. Ali at the camera is explicitly counterfactual. The marsh, ranch fields and labour scenes are not a surveyed Kamiti layout. Keep the unchanged source TIFF separate.

## Royal Ring's specific limits
The original photograph already includes the elephant-mounted king, a working rider and other figures; no claim that those people were visually erased. The wider elephant ring, Nepalese hosts, trackers, guest locations and camera positions are research-led interpretations of a broader expedition, not a recovered negative.
