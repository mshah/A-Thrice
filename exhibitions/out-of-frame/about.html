What does a society choose to commemorate—and what does its photograph leave uncredited?

Out of Frame

Beyond the
celebrated subject.

Out of Frame is a research-led art series built around historical photographs of elite hunting expeditions. Presidents, monarchs and political rulers appear in images designed to mark triumph, courage, authority or status.

The project widens the photograph to investigate a different history: skilled elephant handlers, shikaris, local organisers, camp labour, photographers, guests and the animals at the centre of the spectacle.

The image is an object of its own time. A hunting trophy celebrated by one audience may be disturbing to another. The work does not replace the historical picture with a new certainty. It uses visual reconstruction and detailed annotations to open questions about commemoration and whose contributions become visible.

A series of encounters.

The exhibition is planned as a small set of extensively researched photographic reconstructions from different locations and structures of power. Buffalo Corner begins with the organisation of Roosevelt’s Kamiti visit in 1909: camp labour, transport, ranching and wetland habitat around the trophy portrait. The Royal Ring turns to the Nepalese royal hunt of December 1911, and the ceremony, animals and expertise surrounding George V. The Host and the Trophy examines princely hosting, the elephant establishment and the photographic record in Cooch Behar. All three studies can now be explored through their expanded images and research chapters.
