Photographic case note · Cooch Behar, c. 1896

One photograph.
Two frames.

The same dead rhinoceros appeared in two publications. The photograph was unchanged; what its readers were allowed to see was not.

What entered the frame?

The 1898 magazine print retains the caption and a fuller horizontal plate. The 1900 book print reveals more of the mounted figures and changes the lower framing. Neither published version simply contains the other.

Photographic histories

Not a fourth panorama. A documentary case study in the published photographic frame.

01 / A shared negative, different printed views

Two crops.
Two emphases.

British hunter Peter Burges took a photograph of a dead rhinoceros surrounded by elephants and members of a hunting party in Assam. In 1898, an illustrated account of his hunting experiences in McClure’s Magazine printed the scene with some of the mounted figures cut off above. In 1900, Wild Sports of Burma and Assam printed a different portion of the same exposure, revealing more of the people.

What changes is not the historical event but the printed frame. The later publication does not simply extend the earlier one, and the earlier one does not preserve every element of the later one. They are two related views of the same photograph as it circulated in print.

02 / The words beside the photograph

Two captions.
Two accounts.

1898 / McClure’s “Elephants gathering round a rhinoceros that has just been shot from the howdah.”
1900 / Pollok & Thom “A Dead Rhinoceros.”

The first caption stages an action and identifies where the shot was fired. The later caption is shorter and more static. The frame changes. The words change. Neither publication is neutral.

03 / Evidence & limits

What the frame
cannot settle.

Historian L. C. Rookmaaker links Burges’s photograph to a Cooch Behar hunting expedition associated with Maharaja Nripendra Narayan in 1896, but the exact rhinoceros and exposure date remain unidentified. The people in these reproductions cannot be named confidently from the images alone.

We do not know who chose either publication crop, why, or how much more of the scene the original negative may have contained. This page compares two surviving printed artefacts. It does not claim to recover a lost complete original.
