01 / INSIDE THE RECORD

Why this album exists.

Controlled Burn deliberately refuses the expected sound of an environmental album. Under glossy dance-pop, UK garage and liquid drum-and-bass textures, it follows plastic, parcels, heatwaves, smoke, synthetic fashion, chemical persistence and unequal vulnerability. The narrator is not a detached lecturer: she loves the clothes, accepts the deliveries and keeps dancing even as the warnings arrive.

I wanted to explore the gap between knowing what is happening and living an ordinary pleasurable life inside the systems causing it. The music needs to be seductive enough for that contradiction to be real.

NARRATIVE ARCPleasure and consumption → ambient crisis → invisible persistence → inequality → warning → mortality.

Inside the album

About Controlled Burn

Controlled Burn is an environmental political dance record about crisis becoming ordinary life. Its central contradiction is simple: the planet is becoming less stable, but the beat is really good. The narrator gets dressed, flirts, commutes, orders things, sorts recycling, checks weather apps, turns on fans and air conditioning, pays for offsets and goes dancing. Ecological instability becomes part of the user interface of a normal week.

The narrator is intentionally implicated. She buys the polyester, wants next-day delivery, appreciates good logistics, follows recycling instructions, enjoys flying and still asks whether the dress comes in black. The album is less interested in ignorance than in what happens when knowledge and ordinary appetite coexist inside the same person.

The deepest material motif is persistence. Blue Bin introduces the desire for “away.” Polyester Girl pairs fast identity churn with durable synthetic material. Microplastic turns fragmentation into unwanted intimacy. Forever Chemicals turns romantic permanence into environmental threat. Biodegradable closes by asking whether humans being able to break down is not a defect but part of what makes life meaningful.

Controlled Burn extends A-Thrice’s earlier systems work. Citizen of Empire: Empire Infrastructure asked how history survives as routes, customs systems, supply chains and software. Here routes deliver parcels, carry smoke across borders and move persistent chemistry downstream. Panic by Design examined alerts, fine print and procedural calm; Controlled Burn repurposes that grammar for environmental warnings and claims. The Experiment Continues contributes a looser resonance around maintenance and tomorrow: continuation is not proof that a system is healthy.

The arc moves from pleasure toward afterlife. Early tracks stay close to nightlife and consumer interfaces. Smoke Season pushes the atmosphere into the room. Microplastic, Carbon Neutral and Forever Chemicals deepen contamination, language and persistence. Loss & Damage asks who can afford to adapt. Weather Warning turns catastrophe into an irresistible notification stream. Biodegradable then removes the machinery until the record ends not with a policy slogan but “So dance with me.”

Recurring images across the album

AWAY: a place that is not a place

Blue Bin treats “away” as transfer of ownership (“out of mine”). Microplastic asks where away physically is. Forever Chemicals shows matter reappearing downstream. Biodegradable finally says: “I wanted away / like it was a place / instead of a phrase.”

FOREVER: promise → threat → perspective

Polyester Girl makes “forever” a timescale mismatch; Microplastic makes it a relationship joke; Forever Chemicals makes it the horror; Biodegradable refuses the assumption that permanence is always desirable.

ROUTES: empire → parcels → smoke → molecules → waste

Citizen of Empire established routes as infrastructure. Controlled Burn sends smoke across customs, parcels through optimized networks, chemicals downstream and waste between hands. “Change the route / change who cares” asks whether logistics can solve an objective problem.

INSIDE / OUTSIDE

Controlled Burn says stay inside while a venue may not have air; 39° says home is hotter than the street; Smoke Season closes windows; Loss & Damage shows filtered hotel air beside power failure; Weather Warning says stay indoors and still go to work. “Inside” becomes a resource, not an automatic refuge.

COLORS: blue / green / orange / amber / red

Blue means sorting, green means virtue, orange/amber/red mean risk, and smoke turns the sky into the same palette. Weather Warning’s “Everything’s a colour now” exposes both the usefulness and saturation cost of status coding.

CARRY ON

The phrase moves from human routine under warning to a Panic by Design-like institutional imperative and finally, in Biodegradable, to matter that “changes state / and carries on.”

CLEAN

Blue Bin wants clean packaging; Carbon Neutral wants a clean label; Forever Chemicals creates visibly clean surfaces while chemistry lingers; Loss & Damage says adaptation sounds “clean”; Biodegradable imagines true transformation into “something clean.”

TOMORROW

Next Day Delivery sells tomorrow as convenience. 39° says tomorrow brings the same heat. Weather Warning implies another workday. Biodegradable says tomorrow “isn’t here yet,” making uncertainty a reason for presence.

DESIRE AFTER KNOWLEDGE

The narrator knows and still participates: she goes out under warning, buys polyester, admires logistics, pays for an offset and asks what color comes next. Information never grants instant moral purity.

THE PARTY ARC

At the start, dancing can look like avoidance: “Everything is normal / if you don’t look up.” At the end, “So dance with me” arrives after the listener has looked up, downstream, at fine print and at unequal exits. Dancing becomes presence rather than ignorance.

Connections across the A-Thrice catalogue

Citizen of Empire / Empire Infrastructure

Primary bridge: infrastructure, routes, customs, supply chains, software, mobility and the question of what systems are designed to do. Strongest connections: Smoke Season, Next Day Delivery, Loss & Damage, Biodegradable.

Panic by Design

Primary bridge: alerts, clean interfaces, fine print, procedural calm and signal-versus-noise. Strongest connections: Controlled Burn, Carbon Neutral (“*Terms may apply”), Weather Warning, Loss & Damage.

The Experiment Continues

Primary bridge: maintenance, continuation and tomorrow as responsibility rather than guaranteed progress. These are mostly thematic resonances rather than direct lyrical callbacks; strongest in 39° and Biodegradable.
