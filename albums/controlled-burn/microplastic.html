Microplastic

“Microplastic” turns contamination into relationship language.

Listen / buy this song on Bandcamp ↗
About this song

“Microplastic” turns contamination into relationship language. The first lines could describe an unwanted lover or intimacy that crossed a boundary: “You got in somehow / I don’t remember / letting you.” Only gradually does the song reveal clothes, dust, water, food and microscopic fragments. The environmental idea arrives through the body before it arrives as an argument.

The song is built around the paradox of fragmentation. “Break you down / You just get harder to see” sounds like successful disposal until visibility is separated from existence. Smaller is not the same as gone. The bridge makes that point through a disappearing sequence—bottle, bag and dress vanish from view while material remains in another form.

“They found you in the blood” refers to published studies reporting plastic particles or polymers in human blood. Detection should not be inflated into proof of a particular disease mechanism: exposure and health effects remain active research areas, and measurement quality is still being refined. The song’s claim is narrower and more intimate: material once imagined as “outside” has been detected inside human systems.

The song also deepens the album’s word “away.” Blue Bin used “out of sight / out of mine” to describe delegated responsibility; here the same idea becomes physical. “Where is away / when nothing goes away?” becomes one of the album’s thesis questions, later answered—and inverted—by Biodegradable.

Artist’s annotations

Choose an underlined lyric, or open an annotation below.

01“You got in somehow / I don’t remember / Letting you”

Relationship metaphor

The opening withholds the environmental subject and frames contamination as a boundary violation inside relationship language. “Letting you” carries the language of consent without claiming that exposure is a deliberate interpersonal act.

02“Came home with me on my clothes / … / Followed everything I washed”

Factual context + internal callback

Synthetic textiles are one documented source of microfibres released during washing and wear. The line directly continues Polyester Girl, where fibers went down the drain.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
03“In the water by my bed / In the dust I breathe at night”

Factual context

Microplastics have been reported in water, food and airborne dust. The setting is deliberately domestic rather than remote: the “environment” is beside the bed and inside the home.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
04“Break you down / You just get harder to see”

Thesis metaphor

The line distinguishes fragmentation from elimination. A larger plastic item can become smaller fragments without ceasing to exist as material. Disappearance from sight is not disappearance from the system.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
05“I never said forever / You took it literally”

Internal foreshadowing

Persistence becomes a clingy relationship joke. It also foreshadows Forever Chemicals, where “forever” becomes the explicit subject. Across the album, romantic permanence changes from sweet language into material threat.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
06“Now you’re part of everything”

Factual guardrail

This is poetic compression, not a scientific claim that every object or organism contains microplastic. It captures the breadth of reported distribution across water, air, soils, food webs and human samples while remaining inside the obsessive-lover metaphor.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
07“They found you in the blood / So I guess you made it through”

Scientific context / guardrail

Published studies have reported plastic particles or polymer signals in human blood. Detection alone does not establish a particular health effect, and methods for measuring microplastics in human tissues remain an active quality-control issue. The lyric’s inference is simply that an “outside” material crossed into an “inside” sample.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
08“Bottle gone / Bag gone / Dress gone / Still here”

Direct internal callback cluster

The list gathers earlier album objects: bottle from Blue Bin, dress from Polyester Girl, bag from packaging/delivery. Their consumer identities disappear while the material motif persists.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
09“Down the drain / Out to sea / Back around / Back to me”

Factual guardrail + route motif

The rhyme creates a circular route rather than a one-way disposal chain. It does not claim every particle follows this exact path back to the same person; it dramatizes connected waste streams and ecosystems.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
10“Throw away / Such a funny phrase / Where is away / When nothing goes away?”

Album thesis

This is a core album thesis. “Away” is exposed as grammatical convenience with no visible destination. Blue Bin made “away” a transfer of ownership; Microplastic makes it a material problem; Biodegradable later makes it a mortality problem.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
11“I thought we were disposable / You disagreed with me”

Double meaning

The pronouns work as breakup joke and material critique. A product can be disposable to the consumer while its material is not disposable to the environment.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
12“I threw you away / You didn’t go”

Outro meaning

The ending strips the song to its simplest contradiction. Throwing something “away” ends possession; it does not guarantee material disappearance.

Reading and references
  • UNEP — microplastics and synthetic textile microfibres
  • World Health Organization — micro- and nanoplastic exposure / research gaps

Further artist notes
  • Voice distinction: relationship language carries the environmental idea; the artist voice is strongest in the “away” bridge.
  • Satire target: the concept of disposable material, not people who use plastic under ordinary conditions.
  • Scientific guardrail: detection in blood/tissues is not the same as proof of a specific health outcome.
  • Bottle + bag + dress is a deliberate collection of earlier objects. “Where is away?” becomes the question Biodegradable resolves.
  • “Forever” points forward to Forever Chemicals; “disposable” points forward to the closer’s human reversal.
