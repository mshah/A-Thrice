Smoke Season

“Smoke Season” widens Controlled Burn from the household and street to geography.

Listen / buy this song on Bandcamp ↗
About this song

“Smoke Season” widens Controlled Burn from the household and street to geography. Canada, San Francisco, Spain, France, England and Sumatra enter one summer vocabulary of air-quality checks, sealed windows, alerts and altered plans. The song deliberately does not claim that all of these fires share identical causes; its comparison is experiential: more places are learning what it means to organize ordinary life around recurring smoke.

The chorus deliberately sounds like a beautiful summer track. “Pretty sky, bad signal” catches the contradiction: smoke can produce spectacular light while indicating dangerous air. “Best summer / We’ve had yet” belongs to holiday and nightlife language even as the surrounding verse describes severe fire risk. The record refuses to separate pleasure cleanly from the conditions surrounding it.

The line “Doesn’t stop for customs, babe” is a particularly strong bridge to Citizen of Empire. That earlier project is full of borders, customs systems, passports and routes that sort human movement. Smoke ignores those systems completely. Political geography remains real for people; atmospheric geography does not honor paperwork.

The final “Probably both” refuses the comfortable choice between sunset and smoke, beauty and danger, ordinary summer and abnormal climate. By the end, the frightening development is not only haze but how quickly “smoke season” can sound like a normal calendar entry.

Artist’s annotations

Choose an underlined lyric, or open an annotation below.

01“Crosses borders, clears the gate / Doesn’t stop for customs, babe”

Strong canon echo

Wildfire smoke is transboundary pollution: it moves with atmospheric conditions rather than passports. The joke personifies smoke as a traveler that bypasses customs, a conceptual inversion of Citizen of Empire’s border machinery.

02“Air purifier by the door / San Francisco used to breathe / Now we check before we leave”

Factual context

San Francisco’s traditionally mild coastal climate makes new household filtration/cooling rituals especially visible. “Used to breathe” is nostalgic shorthand, not a claim that the city once had perfect air. The behavioral change—checking air before going outside—is the point.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
03“Pretty sky, bad signal”

Double meaning

Smoke can scatter light into dramatic orange/red skies while degrading air quality. “Signal” preserves the album’s interface language: the eye says beautiful while the data says danger.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
04“Burning season / Used to have a place / Now it follows summer / Everywhere we stay”

Factual guardrail + thesis

The phrase begins from regions where seasonal smoke and haze have long been part of the calendar, including parts of Southeast Asia. The song globalizes the lived experience without claiming identical fire causes. “Used to have a place” is about collapsing imagined distance.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
05“Spain is packing up again”

Wordplay

“Packing up” is ambiguous between holiday travel and evacuation. Emergency behavior is phrased like ordinary summer movement, allowing leisure and danger to occupy the same vocabulary.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
06“France made weather out of flame”

Factual context

Intense wildfires can generate convective clouds, including pyrocumulonimbus systems, with dangerous wind/lightning effects. The line sounds mythic because fire crosses from being acted on by weather into participating in weather-like behavior.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
07“Now the phone says / Very high risk / Don’t light anything / Please don’t”

The clipped language reflects real warning styles while “Please don’t” turns institutional instruction into an exhausted human plea. The satire is not aimed at safety advice; it is aimed at how quickly extraordinary conditions become lifestyle guidance.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
08“No fireworks / No barbecue / Take a picnic / That’s the rule”

Factual reference

London Fire Brigade has explicitly recommended taking a picnic rather than using disposable barbecues during dry fire-risk periods. The humor comes from scale mismatch: wildfire risk produces a modest change in lunch plans.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
09“Best summer / We’ve had yet”

Performance meaning

The line should not sound like a sneer. “Best summer” belongs to the language of long evenings and outdoor pleasure, while adjacent lines contain alerts and red skies. Enjoyment and danger can be sincere at once.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
10“Sumatra / Canada / Spain and France / Different maps / Same planet / Same haze”

Factual guardrail

“Different maps” is a built-in guardrail against flattening distinct fire ecologies, land-use histories and ignition causes. “Same planet / same haze” shifts the comparison from causation to atmosphere and adaptation.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
11“We don’t call it strange / For long these days”

Internal callback

This is the song’s thesis about shifting baselines. Adaptation is necessary, but it can also erase the emotional signal that something fundamental changed. It echoes “Like repetition kills the fear” from the opener.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
12“Check tomorrow / Probably both”

The song refuses to choose between sunset and smoke. “Both” becomes a miniature statement of the album’s refusal of clean categories: beautiful/dangerous, ordinary/abnormal, pleasure/crisis.

Further artist notes
  • Voice distinction: traveler/resident adapting plans; the artist voice appears through geographic accumulation.
  • Satire target: normalization, not warning agencies or people following safety advice.
  • Factual guardrail: Sumatra, Canada and European fires should not be described as one causal phenomenon. The shared layer is smoke, seasonality, transboundary air and adaptation.
  • Canon bridge: customs and borders are Citizen of Empire machinery; smoke’s indifference to them makes atmosphere a counter-system.
  • Production symbolism: “Burning season” should be especially beautiful. Beauty is not a contradiction to remove; it is the point.
