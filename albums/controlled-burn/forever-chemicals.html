Forever Chemicals

“Forever Chemicals” is the romantic inverse of Carbon Neutral.

Listen / buy this song on Bandcamp ↗
About this song

“Forever Chemicals” is the romantic inverse of Carbon Neutral. Instead of joking about labels that make a problem disappear, it takes “forever”—usually a promise of love, stability or quality—and makes persistence frightening. “You said forever / like it was a nice thing” is both breakup language and material-science language.

The verses move through ordinary uses associated with PFAS chemistry: water- and stain-resistant surfaces, non-stick performance and firefighting foam. The “little miracles” matter because persistence entered consumer life through genuine usefulness. The song is not about people choosing an obviously pointless substance; it is about desirable technical properties with difficult environmental afterlives.

“Forever chemicals” is a broad public nickname for PFAS, a very large class. Persistence and toxicology vary across individual substances, so the lyric should not be annotated as if every PFAS behaves identically. The metaphor remains sound: many PFAS are highly resistant to degradation, and certain well-studied PFAS are associated with established health concerns.

The final reversal—“We made forever / for things we throw away”—is one of the album’s central material arguments. Industrial culture paired short use cycles with durable chemistry. Biodegradable later answers this by suggesting that perhaps the human capacity to break down is not a design failure at all.

Artist’s annotations

Choose an underlined lyric, or open an annotation below.

01“You said forever / Like it was a nice thing / I believed you”

Relationship metaphor

The song opens in romantic grammar. “Forever” usually signals devotion and reliability; the track slowly makes those same properties threatening.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
02“Rain rolled off, I stayed dry / … / Nothing stuck”

Factual context

Water repellency and non-stick performance are consumer functions historically associated with fluorinated chemistries. The lyric foregrounds usefulness so the politics does not depend on pretending the products had no benefit.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
03“Little miracles everywhere / Made the mess just disappear / Didn’t ask what lingered there”

Internal motif

Visible cleanliness can conceal material persistence. This repeats Blue Bin’s structure: the surface looks resolved while the back end remains active.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
04“Nothing stains / Nothing sticks / Nothing breaks / Nothing quits”

Wordplay

Product benefits become personality traits. “Doesn’t quit” sounds admirable until persistence becomes environmental. The line exposes how product language often stops at the performance window we care about.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
05“Forever chemicals”

Scientific guardrail

A public nickname for PFAS. PFAS is a broad class, not one substance; persistence, mobility, bioaccumulation and toxicity vary. The annotation should preserve the nickname without treating every PFAS as identical.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
06“Until forever / Got inside of me”

Scientific context

Certain PFAS have been widely detected in human blood and some have established health concerns. As in Microplastic, “inside” is the key metaphor; the song need not claim a specific disease outcome.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
07“Fire foam along the ground / White as snow around the base”

Factual context

Aqueous film-forming foam (AFFF), especially in aviation, military and petrochemical fire response, has been a significant PFAS contamination source at some sites. “White as snow” makes the scene visually innocent.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
08“Washed away into the stream / Turned up somewhere else again”

Internal motif

“Washed away” sounds like removal until “somewhere else” reveals relocation. This extends the album’s “away” critique from trash to dissolved/mobile chemistry.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
09“We were very good at making / Things that travel very far”

Strong canon echo

The line connects chemistry to A-Thrice’s route obsession. Citizen of Empire treated routes as political infrastructure; Next Day Delivery treats routes as optimized logistics; here molecules become travelers through water systems.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
10“Waterproof / Stain-resistant / Non-stick / Persistent”

Product-language satire

The bridge sounds like product copy until “persistent” reveals the hidden specification. A performance advantage and environmental liability sit in the same list.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
11“Make it once / Use it quick / Throw it out / It doesn’t quit”

Internal parallel

Short use is paired with long persistence, the same timescale mismatch as Polyester Girl. “Doesn’t quit” is the dark cousin of “made to last.”

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
12“We made forever cheap / For landfills to keep / Then it turns up downstream”

Scientific guardrail + thesis

This does not mean landfills hold every PFAS permanently; PFAS can move through leachate and water pathways. “Cheap” means persistence became embedded in mass-market disposable systems.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
13“We made forever / For things we throw away”

Internal setup

The song’s thesis: disposable behavior was paired with durable performance. Biodegradable later turns the value judgment around and asks whether breaking down was the more elegant design principle.

Reading and references
  • U.S. EPA — PFAS uses, persistence, drinking-water pathways and AFFF context
  • National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine — PFAS exposure / health guidance
  • Public-health and environmental literature on PFAS mobility and persistence
Further artist notes
  • Voice distinction: breakup ballad and product specification coexist; the “you” is chemistry, convenience and the promise of permanence at once.
  • Satire target: the desire to make permanence cheap and ubiquitous, not every use of fluorinated chemistry or every consumer.
  • Scientific guardrail: PFAS is a class containing many substances; avoid universal claims about identical toxicity or behavior.
  • Album architecture: “forever” changes from polyester durability to unwanted intimacy to mortality. Biodegradable depends on this track making permanence emotionally undesirable first.
  • “Things that travel very far” moves the A-Thrice route motif from people and parcels to molecules.
