Listen / buy this song on Bandcamp ↗
About this song

“39°” takes the album’s heat motif out of the weather app and puts it directly on the body. On first listen it is a humid flirt song: open windows, wet hair, melting ice, bare feet, steamed-up buses and two people staying out because the bedroom is even hotter. The lyric never needs to announce “climate change”; temperature, sleeplessness and the night that refuses to cool do the work.

The central device is a gradual change in what “hot” means. Early lines can be heard as sexual heat and summer-night excitement. Later, “twenty-nine / at midnight,” “nothing’s cooling down” and “tomorrow says / same again” make the same heat feel less romantic. The final chorus changes only a few words—“Everything feels right” becomes “Nothing feels quite right”—allowing the song itself to acclimatize and then become uneasy.

The title supports an additional geographic reading raised by the artist. Lake Tahoe lies at roughly 39 degrees north; the California–Nevada boundary’s angle point in Lake Tahoe is historically tied to the 39th parallel; NOAA also notes latitude-observation sites in Gaithersburg, Maryland and Ukiah, California along that parallel. The lyric does not state this meaning, so it remains a secondary resonance rather than confirmed authorial intent.

As track two, “39°” keeps the party alive while making the crisis physical. Controlled Burn begins with warnings; “39°” asks what it feels like after the warning becomes the room, the pavement, the sheets and the night air.

Artist’s annotations

Choose an underlined lyric, or open an annotation below.

01“39°”

Secondary reading

Primary reading: 39 degrees Celsius is extreme heat. Secondary reading: 39 degrees is also latitude. Lake Tahoe sits around 39°N; the California–Nevada boundary is historically defined through the 39th parallel at Lake Tahoe; and NOAA notes old latitude observatories at Gaithersburg, Maryland and Ukiah, California along that parallel. Because the lyric names none of these places, this is an artist-raised geographic resonance, not “proof” of intent.

02“Fan’s been running / Doing nil”

A cooling appliance becomes a tiny image of inadequate adaptation. The fan is technically functioning—it moves air—but may not meaningfully lower an overheated room’s temperature. The joke is that the device works while the outcome does not.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
03“City kept the heat / From yesterday”

Factual context + metaphor

This compresses the urban heat-island effect into a relationship-song phrase. Built surfaces absorb and re-radiate heat, and warm nights reduce the chance for buildings and bodies to recover. The personification makes the city a storage system: the environment has memory.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
04“Everything is sticking / Everything feels right”

Double meaning

The chorus exploits the overlap between thermal discomfort and sexual attraction. “Sticking” can mean sweat and humidity as well as bodies close together. The politics depends on the seduction being real: the listener should enjoy the heat before realizing the heat is the problem.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
05“If the world’s getting hotter / Then it’s our turn”

Direct internal callback / documented writing intent

This line was deliberately held back during the writing of “Controlled Burn” for use in a later song. In “39°” it works first as a euphoric flirt line. “Our turn” can also mean a generational turn: this is now the heat this generation is inheriting.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
06“Don’t go home yet / It’s hotter inside”

Internal foreshadowing

The usual summer-song logic is reversed. Home should be refuge from outdoor weather; here the flat is less bearable than the street. The line also anticipates Loss & Damage: “inside” is not universally safe, cool or affordable.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
07“Nobody’s sleeping / So nobody leaves”

Double meaning

The line initially sounds like a successful party. Its darker reading is that nightlife has been extended by bedrooms too hot to sleep in. Pleasure and adaptation become indistinguishable.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
08“Was it always / Like this in July?” / “I say, ‘Don’t remember’”

Shifting-baseline reading

The exchange captures normalization through uncertain memory. People compare current extremes to an elastic personal idea of “summer.” “Neither of us tries” matters: the characters do not open a dataset. They return to the night.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
09“Twenty-nine / At midnight”

Factual context + performance meaning

This is the moment the flirt song quietly stops behaving like a conventional summer anthem. A night that remains near 29°C offers little recovery. The performance deliberately gives the number space so it can land without explanation.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
10“Used to wait / For summer / All year … Now it’s here / And nobody / Can sleep”

The nostalgia is not simply for cold weather; it is for a stable seasonal expectation. “Funny” keeps the tone light while the underlying emotion becomes grief for an ordinary pleasure that has become harder to enjoy.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
11“Everything is sticking / Nothing feels quite right”

Structural meaning

The final chorus deteriorates by only a few words. “Feels right” becomes “doesn’t feel quite right.” That small mutation mirrors acclimatization: environmental change can be gradual enough that the language of normality lags behind it.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
12“Tomorrow says / Same again”

Strong thematic echo

Tomorrow is personified as another forecast interface. It also loosely resonates with The Experiment Continues’ interest in what the next day inherits. Here continuation is not automatically hopeful; the system’s output is recurrence. Biodegradable later answers with “while tomorrow isn’t here yet.”

Further artist notes
  • Temperature is the primary title meaning. The 39th-parallel layer is a secondary geographic resonance and should remain labeled that way unless the artist later confirms it.
  • A serendipitous extra layer: NOAA’s historic 39th-parallel observatories were part of measuring Earth’s polar motion or “wobble.” It fits the record’s instability/measurement language without needing to be claimed as planned.
  • The chorus’s near-identical final rewrite is the song’s structural trick: the environment changes by degrees, so the lyric does too.
  • “If the world’s getting hotter / then it’s our turn” was intentionally saved from the opener’s writing process; “hotter inside” foreshadows Loss & Damage; “tomorrow says same again” is answered by Biodegradable.
