Polyester Girl

“Polyester Girl” disguises a material, labor and waste critique as one of the album’s glossiest fashion songs.

Listen / buy this song on Bandcamp ↗
About this song

“Polyester Girl” disguises a material, labor and waste critique as one of the album’s glossiest fashion songs. The narrator wants the clothes, understands scarcity marketing and social-media aesthetics, sees the production questions and still asks, “Does it come in black?” Knowledge does not grant her a clean moral exit from desire.

The song moves between “one size” aesthetics, online scarcity, hidden garment labor, synthetic fibers shed during washing and the historical destruction of unsold luxury inventory. The bridge deliberately jumps from cheap disposability to expensive scarcity: opposite price points can produce similar waste when protecting desire matters more than preserving the object.

The title personifies polyester as a “girl” who is fashionable for a moment but materially persistent. That creates the timescale joke: “New me every Friday / Same fibre lasts forever.” Human identity and trend cycles are fleeting; the synthetic material can remain long after the photograph and season have moved on.

“Made to last / Longer than me” introduces mortality into what began as a shopping song. That line becomes an explicit callback in Biodegradable, where the album finally asks what it means that the supposedly disposable human is more temporary than many materials designed to be thrown away.

Artist’s annotations

Choose an underlined lyric, or open an annotation below.

01“New drop / New me / Same little bag / Same delivery”

Identity is refreshed faster than the delivery infrastructure around it. “New me” promises transformation; “same little bag” reveals repetition underneath novelty.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
02“One size only, looked my size”

Factual / cultural context

The line draws on the exclusionary “one size” fashion aesthetic associated with Brandy Melville. Because the narrator appears to fit, exclusion can remain invisible from inside the benefit.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
03“One size fits the photograph / Crop the edges, make it match”

Strong canon echo

“One size fits all” becomes a rule for image production. If reality does not fit the aesthetic, the frame can be cropped. This connects to A-Thrice’s performance-versus-authenticity motif: the feed constructs what counts as visible normality.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
04“New season / New colour / New version of me”

Systems metaphor

Fashion is framed as identity software: a version update rather than merely a garment. The human self is designed for rapid revision while the underlying synthetic material is not.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
05“Polyester girl / Made to last forever / Wear me for the weekend”

Album motif

The chorus rests on a timescale mismatch: brief desire, long-lived material. “Forever” first sounds like a product virtue or romantic promise and gradually becomes a problem, preparing Forever Chemicals.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
06“Made in Italy / Made invisibly”

Factual context

The rhyme separates prestige geography from labor visibility. Documentary/reporting on Prato has highlighted Chinese migrant labor inside Italy’s garment sector. The line does not claim “Made in Italy” inherently means exploitation; it asks what an origin label can leave unseen.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
07“Model employee / Employee model”

Wordplay

“Model” changes grammatical function. A model employee is exemplary; an employee model becomes part of the brand’s visual merchandise. The wordplay collapses labor and advertising.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
08“Cold wash, delicate / Fibres down the drain / Tiny little pieces / Coming back again”

Factual context + internal setup

Synthetic textiles can shed microfibres during washing. The line deliberately hands the album to Microplastic, where “tiny little pieces” becomes the central relationship metaphor.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
09“Full price / Funeral pyre”

Historical context / guardrail

The bridge references the historical luxury-industry destruction of unsold inventory to protect scarcity or brand value. Burberry publicly ended destruction of unsaleable products in 2018; EU rules beginning in 2026 restrict destruction of unsold apparel for large enterprises, subject to exceptions. Keep the historical tense clear.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
10“Too cheap / Throw away / Too precious / To go on sale”

Apparently opposite market positions converge on waste: cheap replacement at one end, protected scarcity at the other. The material outcome can rhyme even when the pricing logic does not.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
11“One size fits the feed / Not everybody / Just everybody you see”

Signal-versus-noise layer

The feed replaces the body as the standard of fit. A curated stream can create the illusion of universality by filtering counterexamples out of the visible sample.

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
12“I know what it’s made of / Does it come in black?”

Internal callback

The central complicity joke. Information does not automatically extinguish desire. The same structure returns in Next Day Delivery’s “Do I need it? … What colour?”

LINK TO THIS NOTE ↗
13“Made to last / Longer than me”

Direct internal callback

The outro turns durability into mortality. Biodegradable repeats these exact words and calls the achievement a “funny kind of victory.”

Reading and references

  • IUCN — primary microplastics / synthetic textiles
  • Brandy Hellville & The Cult of Fast Fashion, dir. Eva Orner
  • TIME / The Guardian — reporting on Brandy Melville, Prato and fast fashion

Further artist notes
  • Voice distinction: a fashion consumer who understands more than the ad wants her to know and still wants the product.
  • Satire target: churn, scarcity, narrow body aesthetics, hidden labor and material afterlife—not fashion pleasure itself.
  • Factual guardrail: the luxury-fire material is historical; do not imply Burberry currently destroys inventory.
  • Album bridge: this song gives “forever” its first full material sting and ends on the exact line Biodegradable later reuses.
  • “One size fits the feed” is not only body politics; it is also about a platform constructing the sample from which “normal” is inferred.
