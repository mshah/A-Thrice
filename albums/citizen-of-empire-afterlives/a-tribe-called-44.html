About this song

“A Tribe Called 44” is the first hard turn in Citizen of Empire: the moment where A-Thrice stops merely describing misrecognition and starts weaponizing history against it. The song takes the lazy American question “What tribe are you from?” and turns it into a counter-history about empire, migration, racial illiteracy, and the small bureaucratic grammars people use to force complicated lives into primitive categories.

Its key move is the transformation of “Forty-fourth” from punchline into documented reply. The hook invokes the 2017 declaration by President Uhuru Kenyatta recognizing Kenyans of Asian heritage as Kenya’s 44th tribe, honoring the community’s contribution to the nation from its earliest formation. In the song, that recognition becomes both comeback and proof: what sounded to the questioner like contradiction — Indian, African, British, American — is framed instead as the predictable afterlife of empire.

The track also deepens several recurring A-Thrice themes: maps that misfile people, institutional language disguised as common sense, and the difference between being hard to categorize and being hard for broken categories to process. Where “3 Migrations Deep” answers a childhood wound with autobiography, “A Tribe Called 44” answers it with historical swagger.

“A Tribe Called 44”

The title is a deliberate play on A Tribe Called Quest, the influential hip-hop group. The phrase “A Tribe Called…” gives the song an immediate hip-hop lineage, while “44” replaces “Quest” with the Kenyan reference to Asians being described as the country’s 44th tribe.

That makes the title do two things at once: it sounds like a rap homage, and it turns the childhood question “What tribe?” into a historically specific answer.

01“Forty-fourth”
  • Literal meaning: the hook answers the question “What tribe?” with the number forty-four.
  • Reference: this points to Kenya’s 2017 recognition of Kenyans of Asian heritage as the country’s 44th tribe under President Uhuru Kenyatta.
  • Why it matters: the number turns a mocking schoolyard-style question into a documented historical correction.
  • Connection to album / canon: Citizen of Empire repeatedly asks what happens when paperwork, public memory, and personal history collide. This is one of the album’s clearest examples of archive as comeback.
